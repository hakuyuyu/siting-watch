- Named contacts come only from the company's own team pages or the company's own press releases. No emails were guessed or built. The two exceptions are flagged in the CSV:

## Which segments look most promising, and why
1. **Mid-size data center developers.** This is the strongest fit.
   - These firms run many entitlements at once in places where they have no staff: Hanover and Chesterfield VA (Tract), Callaway MO (Crusoe), Oliver County ND (Applied Digital), Temple TX (Rowan), Statesboro GA (4AM), Pinal AZ (Fairview).
   - Moratoria are spreading, and so are by-right-to-SUP ordinance changes (Campbell VA, Rowan NC, Stokes NC, Tucson, Phoenix, Prince William DCOZOD). Late notice now costs real money. Compass's CEO publicly walked away from the 12M SF Prince William project (Bisnow, 2026-05-13).
   - Some firms have entitlement roles you can name, such as PowerHouse's SVP Entitlements. That suggests a real budget owner.
   - Prioritize: Tract, PowerHouse, EdgeCore, Rowan, Crusoe, Applied Digital, Cipher, TeraWulf, Prime, Stream, Sabey, CloudHQ, Novva, and the small single-project developers.
   - Hyperscaler-backed operators (QTS, Vantage, Stack, CyrusOne) are worth a try. They probably have in-house or vendor monitoring already.
2. **Mid-size solar and storage developers.** Strong fit, with lots of seats.
   - Each project goes through county CUPs, road-use agreements, decommissioning agreements, solar ordinance rewrites and moratoria. Fayette IL, Perkins NE, Stafford KS, Baldwin AL, Brookings SD and McLean IL are recent examples.
   - Development managers each cover several counties, and they rely on meeting notice to manage opposition. A per-seat or per-region plan priced at $199–$499 fits.
   - Best accounts: Ranger Power, Swift Current, Geronimo, Doral, Silicon Ranch, Longroad, Arevon, Apex, Hecate, Open Road, Lightsource bp US.
   - Risk: the industry is under financial stress after the tax-credit cuts (see Pine Gate). Lead with ROI and with avoiding bad outcomes.
   - Cross-sell: Geronimo is now looking at a data center in Cass County ND. Energy developers moving into powered land are a natural upsell.
3. **Land-use and zoning law firms.** High willingness to pay, small numbers.
   - They bill clients for exactly this kind of watching, and they are often counsel of record on the agendas they would be monitoring. Examples: Weissman PC in South Fulton, Withey Morris Baugh in Pinal County, Hirschler in Powhatan, Armbrust & Brown in San Marcos.
   - Pitch it as a white-label or client-alert feed that the firm can pass on to its developer clients.
4. **Site selection consultancies.** Good fit and influential, but few firms.
   - SSG and BLS & Co. publish on data center siting and local pushback (SSG 2026-10-05; BLS 2026-08-06), so they care about early warning of local politics.
   - They can also refer clients, which makes them good channel partners.
5. **CRE brokerages.**
   - The large firms (CBRE, JLL, Cushman & Wakefield, Newmark) have research teams, so they buy slowly and through procurement. Target individual data center land brokers instead.
   - Specialist powered-land brokers such as Greysteel are the better fit. Their deals depend on knowing which county just rezoned or adopted a moratorium.
   - Market context: Avison Young counted about $6B of US data center land purchases in 1H 2026, up 79% year over year.
